RAM Is Expensive Right Now — Here's How to Not Get Ripped Off

· 4 min read

If you’ve gone looking for a RAM upgrade recently and felt like the prices were higher than you remembered — you’re right, they are. It’s not just inflation and it’s not just bad luck. There are real reasons behind it, and once you understand them, you can shop around them a lot more effectively.

Why RAM Got So Expensive

The short version: three companies make almost all the RAM in the world — Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron. When three players control an entire market, prices don’t behave the way they would with real competition.

On top of that, the AI industry has been swallowing memory capacity at a rate nobody anticipated. Data centres building out AI infrastructure need enormous amounts of RAM. That demand is pulling supply away from the consumer market, and the prices reflect it.

We’re also mid-transition from DDR4 to DDR5. Manufacturers are splitting production between two standards at once, which means neither one is as cheap as it could be. DDR5 is expensive because it’s new. DDR4 is getting more expensive because production is gradually winding down. You’re getting squeezed either way.

The Grocery Store Analogy

RAM pricing cycles work a bit like seasonal produce. When there’s a glut — more supply than demand — prices crash and you feel like you’re getting a deal. When something pulls supply away, prices climb fast.

Right now it’s like showing up to buy strawberries when a big restaurant chain has bought out the whole harvest. The shelf isn’t empty, but what’s left costs more than it did last month. And the guy running the store knows you still need strawberries.

How to Actually Shop Smart

Wait for sale windows if you can. Black Friday and Prime Day are consistently the two best times of year to buy RAM. The discounts can be genuinely significant — not just a few dollars, but sometimes 20–30% off. If your PC is functional right now and you just want more RAM, this is the single best move you can make.

Stick with DDR4 if your system supports it. DDR5 carries a premium that isn’t justified for most people yet. The real-world performance difference in everyday tasks is minimal. If you’re upgrading an existing build, check what your motherboard supports — it almost certainly takes DDR4, and that’s the cheaper option right now.

Buy a kit, not individual sticks. RAM works best in matched pairs running in dual-channel mode. A 2x8GB kit will outperform a single 16GB stick and often costs the same or less. Always buy a kit.

Don’t overbuy. 16GB is still the right amount for the overwhelming majority of people. The idea that you need 32GB for normal use is mostly marketing. Be honest about what you actually do on your PC — if you’re not editing video or running virtual machines, 16GB is your answer.

Check the used market. DDR4 RAM from a reputable seller on eBay or local marketplace apps is genuinely low-risk. RAM either works or it doesn’t — there’s no gradual degradation the way there is with a hard drive or battery. If you’re on a tight budget, a used kit from a seller with good feedback is a legitimate option.

Look at lesser-known brands. Samsung, Corsair, and G.Skill carry brand premiums. Brands like Patriot, Kingston, and TeamGroup use the same underlying memory chips and cost noticeably less. The Patriot Viper Steel 16GB DDR4 is a good example — solid kit, lower price than the name brands, does exactly what you need it to do.

What to Actually Buy Right Now

For most people upgrading an existing desktop: a 16GB DDR4 kit at whatever speed your motherboard supports (usually 3200MHz is fine). Don’t pay extra for RGB. Don’t pay extra for a heatspreader that looks aggressive. Pay for the memory, not the aesthetics.

For a new build going on a DDR5 platform: budget for it being more expensive than you expect and factor that into your overall build cost. It’ll come down over time, but right now it’s part of the cost of entry on newer platforms.

Adam’s Take: If you need RAM right now, buy it — but buy smart. Get a 16GB DDR4 kit from a brand that isn’t charging you for the name, skip the RGB, and move on. If you have any flexibility at all on timing, wait for Black Friday. I’ve seen prices move meaningfully during those windows and it’s worth the patience. The market will eventually correct itself the way it always does, but “eventually” doesn’t help you today. Shop the sales, don’t overbuy, and don’t let anyone talk you into 32GB unless you genuinely need it.
