Most people never clean their PC. Not once. They buy it, they use it for years, and they never think about what's happening inside the case. Out of sight, out of mind.

The problem is that your PC is essentially a fan-powered dust collector. It pulls air in constantly to keep itself cool, and everything floating in that air — dust, pet hair, skin cells, whatever — gets trapped inside. Over months and years it builds up into something genuinely alarming.

Here's what that actually costs you.

## Your PC Gets Hotter. A Lot Hotter.

Your computer generates serious heat when it's running. The fans exist for one reason — to move cool air in and hot air out. When dust clogs those fans and coats the heatsinks, that airflow gets blocked. The heat has nowhere to go.

Your CPU and GPU respond to this by throttling — deliberately slowing themselves down to generate less heat. So that PC that used to feel fast starts feeling sluggish, not because anything is broken, but because it's protecting itself from the dust you never cleaned out.

Leave it long enough and the temperatures get high enough to cause real damage. Components degrade faster under sustained heat. Solder joints weaken. Capacitors fail earlier than they should. What started as a free maintenance problem becomes a paid repair problem.

## The Blocked Chimney Analogy

Think of your PC like a fireplace. A fireplace needs a clear chimney to work properly — heat and smoke go up and out, fresh air comes in from below, and everything burns cleanly.

Now imagine never cleaning that chimney. Year after year, soot and debris build up until the airflow is barely a trickle. The fire still burns, but the smoke has nowhere to go. The whole thing runs hotter, dirtier, and less efficiently than it should. Eventually something gives.

Dust in your PC is the soot. Your heatsinks are the chimney. And just like a real chimney, the fix is simple — you just have to actually do it.

## Your Fans Work Harder and Die Sooner

Fans clogged with dust have to spin faster to move the same amount of air. That means more noise — that loud jet engine sound your PC makes under load that it didn't used to make. It also means the fan bearings wear out faster.

A case fan costs $15–$20 to replace. A CPU cooler fan is more. A GPU fan replacement can get complicated fast. None of these are expensive problems on their own, but they're entirely avoidable problems. A can of compressed air costs less than any of them.

## Dust Can Actually Short Circuit Components

This one surprises people. Dust is generally not conductive on its own, but it absorbs moisture from the air. Humid, moisture-laden dust sitting across electrical contacts or circuit board traces can cause shorts. It doesn't happen often, but when it does it's not a cheap fix.

If you live somewhere humid, or your PC lives near a kitchen or bathroom, this is worth taking more seriously than the average person does.

## How Often Should You Actually Clean It?

Every 6 months is the standard answer and it's a good one. If you have pets — especially cats or dogs that shed — bump that up to every 3 months. Pet hair is remarkably good at finding its way into PC cases and wrapping itself around fan blades like it was designed to.

If your PC is on the floor rather than on a desk, it's pulling in more dust from carpet level. Same applies — clean it more often.

## How to Actually Do It

You don't need to be technical to clean a PC. You need a screwdriver to open the case and something to blow the dust out with.

The tool I actually use and recommend is this [electric air blower with a brush attachment](https://amzn.to/3Rdz7Ma) — the brush is the part that makes it genuinely useful. Compressed air cans move dust around, but when you've got dust caked onto a heatsink or wrapped around a fan blade, a brush dislodges it properly. The blower then clears it out. It's a better combination than either tool alone.

The process itself:

- Shut down completely and unplug from the wall
- Take the PC somewhere you don't mind getting dusty — outside is ideal
- Open the side panel
- Blow out the fans, heatsinks, and vents — hold fans still while you do it so you don't spin them too fast
- Use the brush on anything caked on
- Wipe down surfaces with a dry cloth
- Put it back together

That's it. Twenty minutes, twice a year. The difference in temperatures and noise levels after a proper clean is immediate and obvious.

## Laptops Too

Laptops are actually worse for this than desktops because the vents are smaller and more easily blocked. The fan and heatsink are crammed into a tight space and dust packs in densely. If your laptop runs hot, sounds loud, or throttles under load — dust is the first thing to check.

Most laptops can be cleaned by removing the bottom panel and blowing out the fan and heatsink. Some are more involved than others. If you're not comfortable opening yours, this is exactly the kind of thing a local tech can do quickly and cheaply.
