If you've ever been near someone with a mechanical keyboard you've heard it. That satisfying clicky sound every time they type. And if you've ever looked at the price tag on one, you've probably wondered if the people buying them have lost the plot.

They haven't. But the answer to whether you need one is more nuanced than the mechanical keyboard community would like you to believe.

## What's Actually Different?

A regular membrane keyboard works by pressing a key down onto a rubber dome that completes a circuit underneath. It's mushy, it's quiet, and it gets the job done. Most keyboards that came bundled with a PC are membrane.

A mechanical keyboard has an individual physical switch under every single key. That switch has a defined actuation point — the exact moment the keypress registers — and depending on the type of switch, it either clicks, bumps, or glides smoothly as it activates. Every keypress feels deliberate and precise.

The difference in feel is immediately obvious the first time you type on a good mechanical keyboard. Whether that difference is worth the price is a different question.

## The Pen Analogy

Think about the difference between a cheap ballpoint pen from a gas station and a quality pen that actually fits well in your hand and writes smoothly.

The cheap pen works. It gets words on paper. Nobody's arguing otherwise. But if you write for hours every day, the quality pen is less fatiguing, more consistent, and just more pleasant to use. You stop thinking about the tool and just do the thing.

That's mechanical vs membrane in a sentence. Membrane works. Mechanical gets out of the way.

If you type a lot — for work, for gaming, for anything — a mechanical keyboard is a tool upgrade the same way a better pen is. If you type occasionally and it doesn't bother you, the cheap pen is fine.

## Who Actually Benefits From a Mechanical Keyboard?

**Gamers** — this is where mechanical keyboards genuinely shine. The precise actuation point means faster, more consistent keypresses. In fast-paced games where a split second matters, that's real. Most serious gamers use mechanical for a reason.

**Heavy typists** — writers, developers, anyone spending hours a day at a keyboard. The tactile feedback reduces typing errors and the defined actuation means less finger fatigue over long sessions. It sounds like marketing but people who make the switch rarely go back.

**People who just enjoy it** — this is a legitimate reason. Your keyboard is something you interact with every single day. If using it is more satisfying, that has value.

**Casual users** — honestly, membrane is probably fine. If you're checking emails and browsing the web a few hours a day, a mechanical keyboard is a nice luxury but not a meaningful upgrade to your life.

## The Switch Types — Keeping It Simple

Mechanical switches come in three basic flavours and this is where people overcomplicate it:

**Clicky** — makes a distinct click sound and feel when it actuates. Satisfying to type on, loud enough to annoy everyone around you. Razer Green switches fall into this category.

**Tactile** — you feel the bump when the key registers but it's quieter than clicky. Best of both worlds for most people.

**Linear** — smooth all the way down, no click, no bump. Preferred by many gamers for rapid keypresses.

Don't overthink this. If you can try switches before buying, do it. If not, tactile is the safe middle ground for most people.

## Does RGB Actually Do Anything?

No. RGB lighting has zero impact on performance. It looks good, it's fun to customize, and it's become standard enough that avoiding it to save money often doesn't save you much anyway. 

If you like it, enjoy it. If you don't care, look for a version without it — you might save a few dollars.

## What About Wireless?

Wireless mechanical keyboards have gotten genuinely good. A few years ago the latency was noticeable enough that serious gamers avoided them entirely. That's no longer true at the higher end — the Corsair K100 AIR is a good example of wireless done properly. For everyday typing wireless is great regardless. For competitive gaming, wired still has a slight edge but the gap has narrowed significantly.
