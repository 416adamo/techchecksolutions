This is one of those questions that starts arguments at family dinners. Your dad insists that turning it off and on all the time wears it out. Your IT friend says you should restart it every day. Someone online told you to just leave it on forever. Everyone is confident. Almost nobody agrees.

Here's what's actually going on.

## The Case for Shutting It Down

Every time your PC runs it generates heat. Heat is the enemy of electronics — it degrades components gradually over time. A PC that runs 24 hours a day is accumulating heat hours that a PC that gets shut down at night simply isn't.

Fans wear out. They have bearings that degrade over time and running them around the clock speeds that process up. Same with hard drives if you still have one — spinning disks have moving parts that wear with use.

There's also the electricity bill. A desktop PC pulling 100 watts left on overnight every night adds up over a year more than most people realize. It's not enormous but it's not nothing either.

And then there's Windows updates. Leaving your PC on doesn't automatically mean updates install cleanly — sometimes they need a proper restart to finish and a machine that never fully restarts can accumulate pending updates sitting in a half-installed state that quietly causes problems.

## The Case for Leaving It On

Modern components are actually designed to handle extended operation. Servers run for years without being shut down. The hardware in your PC is more robust than people give it credit for.

The startup and shutdown cycle itself puts a small amount of stress on components — the thermal expansion and contraction as things heat up and cool down repeatedly. This is real, though the effect is minor on modern hardware.

Sleep and hibernate modes exist for exactly this reason — they cut power consumption and heat dramatically while keeping your session intact so you can pick up where you left off without waiting for a full boot.

If you're doing something that takes a long time — a big download, a backup, a render — leaving the PC on overnight to finish that job makes complete sense.

## The Light Switch Analogy

There's an old myth that turning a light switch on and off constantly wears out the bulb faster than just leaving it on. With old incandescent bulbs there was a grain of truth to it — the filament does experience stress when heating up from cold. But with modern LED bulbs it's essentially irrelevant.

PC components have gone through the same evolution. The wear-on-startup argument made more sense with older hardware. Modern SSDs, modern processors, and modern RAM don't care nearly as much about power cycles as the components from fifteen years ago did.

The light switch myth persists because it sounds logical. The leave-your-PC-on-forever argument persists for the same reason.

## What Actually Matters

The honest answer is that for most people, neither choice is dramatically wrong. But here's what the evidence actually points toward:

**Restart at least once a week.** This is the one that matters most and gets overlooked in the on vs off debate. Windows needs proper restarts to clear memory, finish updates, and flush temporary files that build up over time. A PC that never restarts gets sluggish and weird. A weekly restart fixes this.

**Use sleep mode for overnight.** If you're stepping away for the night, sleep is the right call for most people. Power consumption drops to almost nothing, your session is preserved, and the PC wakes up in seconds. You get the convenience of leaving it on without the heat and electricity cost of actually running it.

**Shut down if you're away for more than a day.** Going on holiday? Shut it down. No reason to run it while you're not there.

**Shut down during thunderstorms.** A power surge through a running PC is more damaging than through one that's off. If you don't have a good surge protector — and most people don't have as good a one as they think — shutting down during bad weather is free insurance.

## What About Laptops?

Laptops are a bit different. Leaving a laptop plugged in at 100% battery constantly can degrade the battery faster over time. Most modern laptops have battery management settings that stop charging at 80% to address this — worth turning on if yours has it.

For the on vs off question, same rules apply: sleep overnight, proper restart weekly, shut down if you're away.

## The Electricity Question

A desktop PC in sleep mode uses around 1–5 watts. Running at idle it might use 60–100 watts or more depending on the hardware. Over a year, the difference between sleeping your PC every night versus leaving it running is a meaningful amount of electricity. It won't break the bank but it's a real number.

If you're environmentally conscious or watching your power bill, sleep mode overnight is the easy win.

> **Adam's Take:** Restart it once a week, sleep it overnight, shut it down if you're away for more than a day. That's it. The people who insist on leaving it on forever and the people who insist on shutting it down every single night are both spending more energy on this debate than it deserves. The restart is the one that actually matters — a PC that never restarts properly is a PC that gradually gets weird and slow, and most people blame the hardware when it's just Windows needing a proper clear-out. Set a reminder if you have to. Weekly restart, sleep overnight. Done.
